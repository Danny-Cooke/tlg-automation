What is a CV: 
A Curriculum Vitae or CV is a Latin phrase meaning, 'course of life’ . Sometimes our Assessors may ask 
you to supply a CV this is so that we can see what other skills and experience you have in your chosen 
field of expertise.  Our assessors will ask for you r CV if they feel that the activity application lacks the 
necessary evidence of the person who has written the learning material or of the person who is delivering 
the training, for example if you only recently achieved your certificate then the assessors may ask to see 
further evidence of your skills and experience.  Do not be offended if you are asked to upload your CV or 
a Resume for further evidence, our assessors just want to make sure that the person(s) writing or 
delivering the training have the righ t level of knowledge and understanding of the subject.   
Your CV should list your academic achievements, employment history, hobbies, interests and 
references.  Start with listing the most recent work experience and work backwards to the first.  Your 
employment history should tell the reader how long you worked at each place of employment, what your 
job title was, and what your role and responsibilities were during your time there.   
If you are self -employed then you will still need to list the date you started your company/business – 
present and what your role and responsibilities are, whether you employ people or not and reasons for 
becoming self -employed.  Did you leave a job becaus e you were unsatisfied with what was expected of 
you?  What do you enjoy most about running your own business?  
A CV will usually have an opening statement which gives an insight into the type of person you are.  This 
can either be written in the first person or the third person.  This should be  a few sentences and an 
opportunity to tell your prospective employer the reasons why you are the most suitable candidate for 
the job.   
 If you have had lots of different employment experiences, we suggest only listing those work experiences 
that are relevant to the subject matter that you are writing or trying to get accredited or perhaps write up 
a Resume instead.  Alternatively, you may  only want to list the last 10 – 15yrs of work experience, 
especially if you have been in a job for a long time.   
 
What to include on a CV  
• Personal details – name, address contact details and a head shot.  
• Opening statement 
• Educational establishments (from GCSE age upwards)  
• Educational achievements (GCSE’s, A ‘levels, Degrees)  
• Places of employment  
• Other achievements and accomplishments.  – this is your opportunity to shout about your 
achievements and accolades during your time with any employer.  Did you get promoted?  Did 
you introduce a new system?  What did you do that helped you and your fellow colleagues make 
working better?  What did you bring to the company during your time with them?  Did you increase 
sales, turnover or distribution?   
• Other certificates – this can include CPD achievements, or courses that you have completed for 
your own interest.

What is a Resume:  
A Resume is shorter and communicates your most recent and relevant work experience history, unlike a 
CV which is longer and more relevant to academic achievements.  You should avoid adding too much 
information and focus on what your responsibilities were r ather than any results driven achievements.   
What to include on a Resume:  
• Name / contact email and phone number.  
• Head shot / opening statement  
• List your education  
• Skills and certifications  
• List of employment & job title / from and to /   
• Bullet point your role and responsibilities for each job  
• Any other achievements and accomplishments – eg, CPD certificates 
• Hobbies and interests 
 
What Not to put on your CV or Resume:  
• Unprofessional email address.   
• An old phone number – ensure that you have the most recent and up to date number  
• Irrelevant work experience 
• Salary 
• Referee’s details  
• Photographs (only a head shot)  
• Grammatical errors 
• Age  
• Lies – with social media and the internet it is easy for employers to find information about you, 
especially if they suspect that you are lying during the interview process.  
 
Facebook, Instagram and Websites: 
Most employers will do some background research into you, so be careful what you put on social media, 
however if you are running your own business then it is a good idea to include the links to your business 
pages and/or website so that prospective employe rs get a feel for your business and can also see how 
active you are on social media.

Your CV or Resume is a reflection of you, so it is important that it is presented professionally, especially 
if you are applying for a job.   
Ideally a CV shouldn’t be more than 3 - 4 pages whereas a Resume is usually only 1 – 2 sides and is an 
overview of your relevant work experience.
