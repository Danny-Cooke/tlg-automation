CPD and “Level” Terminology
The term ‘Level’ is used to identify a standard of skill or knowledge attained through completing aqualification.  If you believe that your learning material is of the same standard as an Ofqual regulatedqualification, then we would need you to provide evidence that identifies which elements of your coursemarries up with those learning outcomes that you are pitching your course against.  As CPD courses are shorter and smaller than a qualification we would not expect all learning outcomes orelements to be covered, although any learning outcomes that you do identify will need to be of the samestandard as the qualification, to be legitimately use the term ‘Level’.  Levels have now also been split into Award, Certificate and Diploma depending on the amount ofinformation and skill demonstrated by the candidate, will ultimately determine the competency Level of thelearner. 
A Working Example: L3 Phlebotomy 
For example, an Award in L3 Phlebotomy is merely and introduction to the service/treatment and would notrequire any prerequisites, other than the candidate be over 18.  However, to obtain a Certificate or aDiploma then learners must already be working in the Healthcare professions where blood taking isrequired and they must also undertake specific assessments.  CPD courses are shorter and should not be used as a substitute to a qualification but to enhance or expandon skills and knowledge already obtained.  
We have written out one unit standard in L3 Phlebotomy as an example.  You would need to go through each unit standard and create a chart such as the one we have given as anexample to show us where the learning outcomes of your course match those of an Ofqual regulatedqualification of the same Level.  You must also number each of your learning outcomes throughout thelearning material so that the assessor can see which learning outcomes you are referring to.
