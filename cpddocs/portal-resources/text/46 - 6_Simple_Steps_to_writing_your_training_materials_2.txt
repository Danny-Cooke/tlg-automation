6 Simple Steps to Writing and Setting out Your Learning Materials for Beauty 
and Aesthetic Treatments: 
 
1.  Introduction: 
Include a little bit of information about yourself, your business, and your training background.  Learners like 
to know a bit about their trainer to help them feel at ease and reassured in you as their trainer.  
 
Introduce the learning outcomes:  What will the training cover? What subjects will you be covering that are 
necessary to their learning?   What will the candidates be learning – is there a practical demonstration, will 
they be required to arrange their own models?  Do they need to let you know of any learning requirements? 
What are the necessary prerequisite requirements to attend the course?   
  
 
2. Health and Safety / Legislation: 
Summarise each legislation that learners need to be aware of.  This helps them to understand their role 
and responsibilities regarding the different laws.  Keep the legislations brief and to the point, try not to 
overwhelm learners with unnecessary blurb  as H&S is a dry subject at the best of times, so keep the 
information short and to the point. Ensure that the legislations are relevant to the treatment you are training 
them to do, for example, The Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 is only  applicable to 
learners who are learning to plumb in a gas oven or pipes, not beauty or aesthetics.    If you are purchasing 
your manuals, please check that the legislations are up to date before you submit it into us.   
 
Health & Safety: 
This includes information on hygienic practices and how to stay safe when working alone or as a mobile 
therapist.   Include information on the most appropriate sterilisation and sanitation methods used for the 
treatment, ergonomics to reduce backache and RSI.  Code of conduct and professional appearance is also 
suggested in this section, especially for those who are new to the industry , or you have chosen to have no 
prerequisites.   
 
 
3. Anatomy & Physiology: 
Ensure that the information included is relevant to the treatment that candidates will be learning.    Try and 
avoid regurgitating information that they already know, instead build on learners’ knowledge by introducing 
new facts or information that they may not already know about the body/skin.  This helps learners to 
develop their understanding of the treatment and the body/skin to help them become better therapists and 
practitioners so that in turn they can correctly educate their clients.    
 
 
4. Treatment Information: 
As a trainer it is your responsibility to ensure that you give the learners all the information they need to know 
about the treatment.  Give your learners some background information to the treatment and how or why it 
has become popular .  Where did it originate from?  Are there any interesting facts that they need to know?

You will need to include information on consultation and managing client expectations and include an 
example of the consultation form so that candidates can see what they need to be asking specific to the 
treatment.  Include relevant images, especially if there are specific techniques that they need to learn.  
Include treatment planning, pricing and promotional ideas to help get students started once they leave 
your training room.    Training is an investment for many people, therefore candidates will want to know how 
they can recoup the cost of their training.  
 
 
5.  Assessment Criteria: 
This section should include details on any assessments that candidates will be required to complete during 
their training.  State how this will be done, eg, practical demonstrations, completing quizzes etc, you can 
even include the test/questions in this section.  You will need to tell candidates exactly what is expected of 
them in order for them to achieve their certification.  Ideally you want to have candidates practicing on each 
other first, as this helps them to understand how the treatment feels, which will enable them to describe it 
better to their own clients.  Arranging models is also another great way for candidates to practice their skills.   
 
 
6. Conclusion: 
Conclude the learning and recap the learning outcomes so that your students can see that everything has 
been covered.  Invite them to ask questions and give feedback on their experience with you.  How will their 
new treatment/skills/knowledge benefit them and their clients?   
 
This is also your opportunity to upsell and promote other training that you offer.  What treatment can they 
do next?  What is the next natural step in the student’s development?  What can you offer them if they 
rebook?   What other training courses do you offer?  How can they book?  Is there ongoing support from you 
once they have completed their training?  These are all things that candidates need to know before leaving 
you and practicing in their own working environment.   
 
 
 
REMEMBER:  The training material(s) that you give candidates will be their learning tool after they have 
completed the initial training with you.  A well-presented manual gives candidates a good impression of 
YOU as a trainer.   
 
If you choose to purchase your manual please check the following before you send it into us.  
• The legislations are up to date and are relevant to the UK 
• Check the spelling for Americanisms, eg, color and colour 
• Check that any pricing is in £ not $

Are you using a power point?   
A power point is a visual representation of the written learning material.  It is ideal for dyslexic learners or those who 
are not natural readers, it also keep the learners engaged.   
How to make your power point fantastic: 
1. Avoid cramming too much text into one slide – it is better to have more slides with less information as the 
brain can only process small amounts of information each time.   
2. Use images where possible.  
3. Use bullet points or short sentences to highlight specific information you want your learners to remember .   
 
How to avoid Death by Power Point  
1.  Keep to 6 words per slide + 1X image.  
2.  One message per slide 
3.  Use a dark background for contrast – helps to keep learners focussed.   
4. Avoid using red, or yellow text as this can be difficult to read, especially at a distance.    
5. Avoid using long sentences on a slide.   
6. Avoid using text that is too small – use a simple font and an easy-to-read size, such as Arial, Calibri, and 
other Sans Serif fonts to help learners.   
7. DO NOT copy and paste the training manual into a power point, this is not good practice and can make you 
appear passionless about subject that you are training; some learners may feel as though they have been 
duped when presented with a learning manual that is the same as the power point.   
8. Avoid too many distractions on the screen as you want the attention to be on the message to keep the 
learners focussed and engaged.  
 
Couse Agendas and Lesson Plans: 
A course agenda is an overview of on what will be covered in the training, it doesn’t require too much information 
and helps to keep the tutor and learners on track.  A course agenda can be given to learners at the beginning of their 
training so that they can see how the training is structured.   
A lesson plan is more detailed and will require extra information such as how the training can be adapted to suit 
learning needs, or what method of delivery is being used and is usually for the tutors’ reference only to help them 
stay on track.   
It is a good idea to submit one or the other into us when sending in your training materials as the assessors can then 
see how the training is structured and if the hours stated, match the hours on the application.
