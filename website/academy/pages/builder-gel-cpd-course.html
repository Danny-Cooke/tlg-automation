A practical skills-development course for existing beauty and nail professionals. You will revisit nail anatomy and the conditions that mean a service must be adapted, postponed or refused; understand how gel cures, why builder gel carries a heat-spike risk that gel polish does not, and how to control it; and learn to prepare the natural nail, apply a slip layer, build a balanced overlay with a correctly placed apex, create a short extension on a tip or form, and carry out infills and safe removal. Allergy prevention, hygiene, UK legislation, consultation, aftercare and honest advertising are covered throughout.

Course details

Course title
Builder Gel Overlay and Short Extension CPD Course
Delivery
Classroom course at The Looking Glass Beauty School, 4a Roushill, Shrewsbury, with pre-course study and support materials.
Duration
17.5 hours. 2.5 hours of pre-course study, 7.5 guided learning hours on the taught day, and about 7.5 hours for three post-course case studies.
Entry requirements
Aged 18 or over, with a Level 2 Beauty Therapy, Nail Services or equivalent qualification covering manicure, nail structure and safe salon practice. Prior gel polish experience is strongly recommended. Not open to anyone with a known acrylate or methacrylate sensitivity. Before offering the service commercially you must confirm acceptance with your own insurer.
Assessment
A written theory paper of 50 marks (pass mark 80%), a directly observed practical on a suitable adult model (at least 80% overall and every safety-critical criterion met) and three post-course case studies. The certificate is issued only when all three are satisfactory; attendance alone is not sufficient.
Certificate
A course certificate from The Looking Glass Beauty School, issued after the taught day, the practical and the case studies. This is a Continuing Professional Development course, not a regulated qualification.
Tutor
Jessica Jane Preston: VTCT Level 2 NVQ Diploma in Nail Services, Award in Education and Training, fourteen years in the industry, teaching since 2021.

What you will learn

  • Explain the purpose, benefits, limitations and client suitability of builder gel, and compare it with gel polish, hard gel and liquid-and-powder acrylic.
  • Identify the structures of the nail unit and recognise conditions that require the service to be adapted, postponed or refused.
  • Conduct and record a professional consultation, obtain informed consent and create an individual treatment plan.
  • Explain oligomers, monomers, photoinitiators, polymerisation, lamp compatibility, under-curing and the inhibition layer.
  • Explain why builder gel carries a heat-spike risk that gel polish does not, and control it.
  • Select, handle, store and dispose of products and equipment in accordance with risk assessment, COSHH, manufacturer instructions and current UK requirements.
  • Prepare the natural nail without avoidable damage to the plate or living tissue.
  • Apply a slip layer and build a balanced overlay with a correctly placed apex, straight sidewalls and an even C-curve.
  • Create a short extension on a tip or form within the product's stated capability.
  • Infill, rebalance and remove builder gel safely by the method the product requires, and provide appropriate aftercare.

Syllabus

  1. Course specification and scope. What the course covers and does not cover, how the day runs, how you are assessed.
  2. Builder gel: purpose, benefits and professional scope. What builder gel is, who it suits, comparison with gel polish, hard gel and acrylic, service time and pricing.
  3. Nail anatomy, physiology and growth. The structures you must protect, how the nail grows, factors that affect it.
  4. Nail diseases, disorders and recognition. Observe, ask, decide, record, refer. Fungal infection, onycholysis, paronychia, green nail, psoriasis, melanonychia and more, with clinical photographs.
  5. Consultation, treatment planning and consent. The consultation sequence, the treatment plan, informed and ongoing consent, data protection.
  6. Gel chemistry, curing science and the heat spike. Photoinitiators, lamp compatibility, the inhibition layer, under-curing and sensitisation, HEMA and TPO, the heat spike and the exact response.
  7. Nail architecture. Apex, stress area, C-curve, sidewalls, zones, nail shapes and their structural demands.
  8. Kit, equipment, files and product storage. Products, lamps, brushes, tips and forms, files and grits.
  9. Health, safety, hygiene and ergonomics. COSHH and the hierarchy of controls, routes of exposure, cleaning, disinfection and sterilisation, blood and skin-break procedure, ventilation and dust, posture.
  10. UK legislation, insurance, data and advertising. The laws that apply at the desk, public and treatment liability, UK GDPR for client records, ASA rules for honest claims.
  11. Preparing the natural nail. The ten-step preparation and the faults that cause lifting.
  12. Applying builder gel. Slip layer, bead placement, self-levelling, flash cure and full cure, checking from every angle, reading the product as you work.
  13. Short extensions on a tip or form. Fitting and blending a tip, fitting a form, building in layers within the product's capability.
  14. Infills, rebalance, repairs and removal. Reducing the old apex, rebuilding, removal by product category, what never to do.
  15. Longevity, troubleshooting, contra-actions and emergencies. Reading a service that has gone wrong, adverse reactions, the general response.
  16. Aftercare and professional client support. Aftercare advice, the written handout, follow-up.
  17. Assessment. Theory paper (50 marks, pass 80%), observed practical on a model (80% and every safety-critical criterion), three case studies within eight weeks.
