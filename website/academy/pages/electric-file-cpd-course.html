A practical skills course for nail professionals who already apply and remove at least one coating or enhancement system. You will learn how an electric file works (parts, speed, torque, direction, chuck and shank), which bit material, shape and grit does which job, and how speed, pressure, angle and time on one spot produce friction heat and damage and how to prevent it. Through tutor demonstration and supervised practice on cured product and on a model you will prepare the natural nail with a safety bit, reduce and remove gel polish, builder gel, hard gel and acrylic without reaching the nail plate, and shape, refine and finish an enhancement. Dust control, vibration, electrical safety, the decontamination of bits, consultation, UK legislation, aftercare and honest advertising run throughout.

Course details

Course title
Electric File (E-File) Nail Technique CPD Course
Delivery
Classroom course at The Looking Glass Beauty School, 4a Roushill, Shrewsbury, with pre-course reading and support materials.
Duration
15 hours. 1.5 hours of pre-course reading, 7.5 guided learning hours on the taught day, and about 6 hours for three post-course case studies.
Entry requirements
Aged 18 or over, with a Level 2 Beauty Therapy, Nail Services or equivalent qualification covering manicure, nail structure and safe salon practice, and a qualification or CPD certificate in at least one of gel polish, builder gel, hard gel or liquid-and-powder acrylic with current experience of applying and removing it. The course works on those products; it does not teach them.
Assessment
A written theory paper of 38 questions (50 marks, pass mark 80%), a directly observed practical on a suitable adult model wearing an existing coating (at least 80% overall and every safety-critical criterion met) and three post-course case studies. The certificate is issued only when all three are satisfactory; attendance alone is not sufficient.
Certificate
A course certificate from The Looking Glass Beauty School. This is a Continuing Professional Development course, not a regulated qualification.
Tutor
Jessica Jane Preston: VTCT Level 2 NVQ Diploma in Nail Services, Award in Education and Training, fourteen years in the industry, teaching since 2021.

What you will learn

  • Explain what an electric file is, what it can and cannot do, its benefits and limitations, and when a hand file is the right tool.
  • Identify the structures of the nail unit and surrounding skin, and recognise conditions that mean the electric file must not be used or the service adapted, postponed or refused.
  • Conduct and record a professional consultation, obtain informed consent and plan an electric-file service around the product on the nail.
  • Describe the parts and controls of an electric file (speed, torque, direction, chuck and shank), and set it up, check it and maintain it safely.
  • Select the correct bit material, shape and grit for each task, and explain how speed, pressure, angle and time on one spot produce heat and damage.
  • Control dust, vibration and electrical risk in line with COSHH, PUWER, the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations and manufacturer instructions, and decontaminate bits between clients.
  • Prepare the natural nail with the electric file: cuticle-area work with a safety bit at low speed, and de-shining without thinning the plate.
  • Reduce and remove gel polish, builder gel, hard gel and liquid-and-powder acrylic with the electric file, leaving a thin layer over the plate, without heat, grooves or damage.
  • Shape, refine and finish an enhancement with the electric file to a commercial standard.
  • Provide aftercare, manage contra-actions and adverse reactions, and recognise and respond to rings of fire, friction heat and an over-thinned plate.

Syllabus

  1. Course specification and scope. What the course covers and does not cover (no product application, no thickened or diseased nails, no hard skin), how the day runs, how you are assessed.
  2. The electric file: purpose, benefits and professional scope. What it is and is not, hand file or machine, where it came from, service time and pricing.
  3. Nail anatomy, physiology and growth. The structures you must protect and how the plate conducts heat to the bed.
  4. Nail diseases, disorders and recognition. Observe, ask, decide, record, refer. What the electric file may and may not do on each condition, with clinical photographs.
  5. Consultation, treatment planning and consent. Planning the service around what is on the nail; the stop signal; data protection.
  6. The machine: parts, controls and set-up. Control unit, handpiece, chuck and shank; speed and torque; forward and reverse; the pre-use check; choosing a machine.
  7. Bits: materials, shapes and grits. Carbide, diamond, ceramic, sanding bands, silicone; barrel, tapered, cone, safety, flame, needle; grit and the colour code; the bit for each task.
  8. Speed, pressure, angle and heat. Friction heat, the flat bit, working with the rotation, the heat check, rings of fire, reading the dust.
  9. Health, safety, hygiene and ergonomics. Dust from the electric file and its controls, vibration and the hands, decontaminating bits, posture.
  10. UK legislation, insurance, data and advertising. COSHH, PPE and respiratory protection, PUWER, the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations, Electricity at Work, WEEE, UK GDPR, ASA rules for honest claims.
  11. Preparing the natural nail with the electric file. The safety bit at the fold, de-shining without thinning, when hand tools are the answer.
  12. Reducing and removing product. Gel polish, builder gel, hard gel and acrylic by removal category; the thin layer that stays; toenails.
  13. Shaping, refining and finishing the enhancement. Free edge, sidewalls, surface, cuticle zone, under the free edge, finishing.
  14. Machine care, bit care and troubleshooting. The checks, bit decontamination, signs of a failing machine, mobile work, reading a service that went wrong.
  15. Contra-actions, aftercare and professional support. Friction heat, rings of fire, dust, the general response; aftercare after a removal.
  16. Assessment. Theory paper (38 questions, 50 marks, pass 80%), observed practical on a model wearing an existing coating (80% and every safety-critical criterion), three case studies within eight weeks.
