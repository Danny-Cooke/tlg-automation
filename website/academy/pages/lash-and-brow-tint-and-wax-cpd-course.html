A practical skills course for beauty professionals who want to offer eyelash tinting, eyebrow tinting and eyebrow shaping safely and to a professional standard. You will revisit the anatomy of the skin, the hair and its growth cycle, the eye, the eyelids and the eyebrow, and the conditions that mean a treatment is adapted, postponed or refused; learn how an oxidative tint colours the hair, why it can sensitise and why the patch test is non-negotiable; and learn, through tutor demonstration and supervised practice on each other and on a model, to tint lashes and brows with the eyes protected and closed, and to map, wax, tweeze and trim the brows to a shape agreed with the client. Consultation, the patch-test system, hygiene, UK legislation, aftercare, emergencies and honest advertising run throughout.

Course details

Course title
Lash and Brow Tint and Wax CPD Course
Delivery
Classroom course at The Looking Glass Beauty School, 4a Roushill, Shrewsbury, with pre-course reading and support materials.
Duration
15 hours. 1.5 hours of pre-course reading, 7.5 guided learning hours on the taught day, and about 6 hours for three post-course case studies.
Entry requirements
Aged 18 or over, with a Level 2 Beauty Therapy or equivalent qualification covering skin structure and function, facial and eye-area treatments, consultation and safe salon practice. No previous lash or brow experience is required.
Assessment
A written theory paper of 38 questions (50 marks, pass mark 80%), a directly observed practical on a suitable adult model with a recorded negative patch test (at least 80% overall and every safety-critical criterion met) and three post-course case studies. The certificate is issued only when all three are satisfactory; attendance alone is not sufficient.
Certificate
A course certificate from The Looking Glass Beauty School. This is a Continuing Professional Development course, not a regulated qualification.
Tutor
Jessica Jane Preston: VTCT Level 2 NVQ Diploma in Nail Services, Award in Education and Training, fourteen years in the industry, teaching since 2021.

What you will learn

  • Explain what eyelash and eyebrow tinting and eyebrow shaping are, their benefits and limitations, who they suit and when another service or a referral is the honest answer.
  • Describe the structure and growth cycle of the hair, the skin of the eye area and the anatomy of the eye, eyelids and eyebrow, and recognise the conditions that mean a treatment is adapted, postponed or refused.
  • Conduct and record a professional consultation, carry out and record a patch test, obtain informed consent and agree an individual treatment plan.
  • Explain how an oxidative tint colours the hair, why it can sensitise, how shade, developer and timing decide the result, and why the products are restricted to professional use and to clients aged 16 and over.
  • Select, handle, store and dispose of tints, developers, waxes and equipment in line with COSHH, manufacturer instructions and current UK requirements.
  • Prepare the client and the treatment area, protect the eyes and skin, and work hygienically throughout.
  • Tint eyelashes to an even, natural-looking result with the eyes protected and closed, no tint on the skin and no tint in the eye.
  • Tint eyebrows to a shade that suits the client, timed and checked, with the skin kept clean.
  • Map, wax, tweeze and trim the eyebrows to a balanced shape agreed with the client, with the wax temperature tested, the skin supported and the eyelid never waxed.
  • Provide aftercare, recognise and manage contra-actions, adverse reactions and tint in the eye, and set honest expectations about results and how long they last.

Syllabus

  1. Course specification and scope. Lash tint, brow tint and brow shaping as one treatment group; what is not covered (lifts, lamination, henna, extensions, threading); how the day runs and how you are assessed.
  2. Lash and brow treatments: purpose, benefits and professional scope. What each treatment does and does not do, the related services, where they came from, service time and pricing.
  3. Anatomy and physiology. The skin and why eyelid skin is different, the hair and its follicle, the growth cycle, the eye and eyelids, the eyebrow and its muscles.
  4. Skin and eye conditions, contraindications and recognition. Observe, ask, decide, record, refer: eye conditions, skin conditions and the medications and treatments that change the plan, with illustrations.
  5. Consultation, patch testing, treatment planning and consent. The consultation sequence, the patch test step by step and when it is repeated, planning and consent.
  6. How tint works. Oxidative dyes and developers, what decides the result, why tint sensitises, the legal position of the products and the age limit.
  7. Brow design: mapping, proportion and shape. The three lines from the side of the nose, the five basic shapes, what limits the shape.
  8. Kit, equipment, products and storage. The room, the tinting kit, hot and strip wax, tools, COSHH storage and disposal.
  9. Health, safety, hygiene and ergonomics. Routes of exposure, cleaning and disinfection, the between-client sequence, the blood-spot procedure, posture.
  10. UK legislation, insurance, data and advertising. COSHH, PPE, first aid, RIDDOR, electrical and fire safety, waste, the Cosmetics Regulation's conditions for eyelash tints, consumer law, UK GDPR, ASA rules for honest claims.
  11. Preparing the client and the treatment area. Set-up, cleansing, protecting the eyes and skin, the order tint, tidy, shape.
  12. Eyelash tinting. Shade choice, the procedure with the eyes closed throughout, reading the lashes as you work, tint in the eye.
  13. Eyebrow tinting. Choosing the shade, the procedure, the one-minute check, reading the brows as you work.
  14. Eyebrow shaping. Mapping, waxing with the growth on and against it off, one spatula per dip, tweezing, trimming, finishing.
  15. Results, troubleshooting, contra-actions and aftercare. What affects how long the result lasts, what goes wrong and why, reactions and emergencies, aftercare and professional support.
  16. Assessment. Theory paper (38 questions, 50 marks, pass 80%), observed practical on a patch-tested model (80% and every safety-critical criterion), three case studies within eight weeks.
