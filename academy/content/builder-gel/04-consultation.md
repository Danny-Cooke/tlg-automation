## A safe service begins before the product is opened

A consultation is a structured two-way conversation and assessment. It establishes what the client wants, whether the treatment is appropriate, how it should be adapted, and whether the client understands costs, maintenance, limitations and risks. A signed form supports the process; it does not replace observation, judgement or dialogue.

## The sequence

1. Create privacy; confirm identity, age and communication needs.
2. Clarify the desired result: finish, shape, length, occasion, work, budget, maintenance.
3. Ask about previous nail services, lifting, removal methods, reactions and allergies.
4. Collect only health and medication information relevant to safety, and explain how it is protected.
5. Assess every nail and both hands visually and by touch. Compare nails; do not conceal suspicious findings.
6. Identify contraindications and decide: proceed, adapt, postpone or refuse. Explain respectfully without diagnosing.
7. Agree the plan: product system, lamp, shape, length, service time, price, removal and maintenance, aftercare.
8. Explain foreseeable risks: heat sensation, lifting, allergy and sensitisation, harm from picking or forced removal.
9. Obtain treatment consent. Obtain separate, optional consent for photographs; refusing marketing use must not affect treatment.
10. Record products, batch numbers where practical, lamp model, cure times, observations and aftercare. Update the record every visit.

## Consent is informed and ongoing

The client must understand what will be done, the material risks, the alternatives and the right to stop. Check consent again if the plan changes or discomfort occurs. Never use wording like "I accept all responsibility". If consent cannot be freely given, do not treat.

## Scenario to think about

A client asks for long almond nails for a warehouse job, reports that previous gel "always lifts", and has a sore split beside the index nail. Identify the immediate contraindication, the lifestyle issue, the questions you would ask and a realistic alternative plan. Write your answer down; it is discussed on the taught day.
