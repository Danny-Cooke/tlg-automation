## What builder gel is

Builder gel is a light-cured gel supplied at a viscosity thick enough to hold a shape but soft enough to self-level. It sits between gel polish and hard gel: it adds genuine structure and a measurable apex, but is generally softer and more flexible, and, depending on the product, may be soaked off rather than filed off.

Because it is applied in thicker layers than gel polish, two things change. The product has time to move on the nail before it is cured, so you must control where it settles. And more material reacts at once, so the heat released during cure becomes something the client can feel.

## A note on the word "BIAB"

"BIAB" is widely used to mean builder gel in a bottle, but it is a registered trade mark of one manufacturer. This course is titled and advertised as a builder gel course. In teaching and in client records, name the actual product used and its removal category rather than assuming every builder gel behaves the same way.

## Benefits and limitations

| Potential benefit | Professional limitation |
|---|---|
| Adds strength and structure to weak or flexible natural nails | It reinforces a nail; it does not repair disease or replace a damaged plate. |
| Self-levelling formulas reduce filing | Self-levelling also means the product flows into the cuticle area and sidewalls if it is not controlled and cured at the right moment. |
| Can create short extensions on a tip or form | Length capability is product-specific. Check what the manufacturer states. |
| Often softer and more flexible than acrylic or hard gel | Flexibility is a benefit on a natural nail and a weakness at length. |
| May be soaked off where the product allows | Removal category is product-specific: soak-off, file-off or both. Never assume. |

## When another service is the honest answer

- Gel polish suits a client who wants colour only on a healthy natural nail, with no added structure.
- Liquid-and-powder acrylic or hard gel suits a client who wants significant length or who is very hard on their hands.
- No enhancement should be used to conceal a suspected infection, unexplained nail change or active reaction.
