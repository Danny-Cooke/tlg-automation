## How gel cures

Light-cured gels are a mixture of oligomers, monomers, photoinitiators, pigments and additives. The lamp supplies light at wavelengths the photoinitiators absorb; they release reactive species that start chain growth and cross-linking; the gel becomes a solid polymer network. This reaction releases heat. A hard surface is not proof of a full cure: thick layers, the wrong lamp, dirty reflectors, poor hand placement or a shortened time all leave uncured product underneath.

The tacky film after curing is the **oxygen-inhibition layer**. It is normal, it is not monomer "pushed to the top", and it is removed only with the system's cleanser and a fresh lint-free wipe for each nail.

## Lamps

LED lamps emit ultraviolet light too; "LED" describes the light source, not a different process. Use the lamp the manufacturer specifies, the exact time for each product and layer, fingers flat and centred. Wattage is electrical power, not proof of curing dose. Never mix brands and hope.

## Under-curing and allergy

Uncured and under-cured (meth)acrylates sensitise skin. Once sensitised, a person can react to tiny amounts and to future dental or medical materials. HEMA and di-HEMA TMHDC are the usual culprits; products containing them are professional-use only and must be labelled "Can cause an allergic reaction". "HEMA-free" does not mean allergy-free. Prevention: thin layers, product off the skin every time, the compatible lamp, full cure, no bare fingers on the inhibition layer, gloves changed when contaminated. Never use gel as a skin "patch test".

## The heat spike

Builder gel is applied far thicker than gel polish, so far more material cures at once and the heat can be felt as sudden burning under the nail. It is worse on thin or over-filed plates and with high-output lamps. The rule:

1. Tell every client before the first cure what it feels like and to tell you at once.
2. If they report heat, the hand comes out of the lamp immediately. Never tell them to sit through it.
3. Let the nail cool, then return it for the rest of the cure.
4. For the next nail: thinner layer, flash cure or low-heat setting where the product permits.
5. Record the event and what you changed.

## TPO

The photoinitiator TPO is banned in the EU and Northern Ireland from 1 September 2025 and restricted in Great Britain from 15 August 2026, with existing stock allowed until 14 February 2027. Check ingredient lists and confirm reformulated products with your supplier.
