## Strength comes from shape, not thickness

A correctly shaped nail distributes force through its structure; a badly shaped one concentrates force at one point and breaks there, however much product is on it.

## The four things a balanced nail needs

- **Apex**: the highest point, in the back third of the nail, blended smoothly in every direction. Too far forward: the nail snaps at the stress area. Too flat: it flexes and cracks. Too high: bulbous, and it catches.
- **Stress area**: where the free edge leaves the nail bed. Almost every break happens here when it is unsupported.
- **C-curve**: an even transverse curve from sidewall to sidewall along the whole length.
- **Sidewalls**: straight when viewed from above, meeting the natural sidewall with no step and no gap.

Thickness is greatest at the apex and tapers everywhere: whisper-thin at the cuticle, credit-card thin at the free edge.

## Check from every angle

Look down the barrel from the free edge for the C-curve, from the side for the apex, from above for the sidewalls. A nail that looks right from one angle only is not balanced. On the taught day you check every nail this way before the final cure.

## Shapes and what they demand

Round, oval, square, squoval and natural short suit an overlay. Almond needs a well-placed apex and even taper. Coffin and stiletto remove support at the free edge and need a strong apex and a conservative length. Never file into the lateral folds to force a shape.

## Files and grits

Lower numbers are coarser. 80 to 100 grit touches cured product only. The 100/180 combination is the standard enhancement file. 180 to 240 refines. The natural nail meets nothing coarser than 240, and then only to remove shine, with light strokes.
