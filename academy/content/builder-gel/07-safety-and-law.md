## Control the hazard before the glove

The salon's COSHH assessment considers each product, the task, the route of exposure, how often and how much, ventilation, who is at risk and what to do in an emergency. The order of controls: eliminate, substitute, engineer (extraction, ventilation), administer (training, thin application, closed containers, cleaning schedules), and only then personal protective equipment. Routes of exposure are skin, eyes, inhalation of dust and vapour, ingestion at the workstation, and cuts.

## Cleaning, disinfection, sterilisation

These words are not interchangeable. Clean first, always. Porous files, buffers and wooden sticks are single-client. Metal tools are cleaned, dried, inspected and disinfected after every client, and steam-sterilised where the risk assessment or local authority requires. A UV cabinet is a storage aid, not a steriliser. Glass-bead units, hot-air ovens and boiling water are not accepted salon sterilisation.

## Blood or broken skin

Stop. Gloves on. Pressure with clean gauze. No product over broken skin. Contaminated single-use items disposed of by the salon's waste route; reusable tools quarantined for validated processing. Record it.

## Ventilation and dust

HSE's guidance for nail bars identifies dermatitis, asthma and respiratory symptoms as the risks. Keep bottles closed, capture dust at source, do not rely on smell as a warning, do not use a desk fan that blows dust across the room.

## Ergonomics

Supportive adjustable chair, feet flat, forearms supported, wrist neutral, client's hand brought into the lit working area rather than your neck bent to the desk, microbreaks, worn files replaced rather than pressed harder.

## The law, in one table

| Law | What it means at the desk |
|---|---|
| Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 | Risk assessments, safe systems, training, maintained equipment. |
| COSHH Regulations 2002 | Assess and control exposure to products and dust; keep safety data sheets. |
| PPE at Work Regulations 1992 (amended 2022) | Suitable PPE provided free where risk remains, including for limb (b) workers. |
| RIDDOR 2013 | Report specified injuries and diagnosed occupational disease such as work-related dermatitis. |
| Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR | Consultation records and health data are personal data; health data is special-category. Lawful basis, privacy notice, minimal collection, secure storage, set retention. |
| Cosmetic Products Enforcement Regulations 2013 | Buy compliant products from reputable supply; keep labels and batches; act on recalls. |
| Local authority licensing | London boroughs need a Special Treatment Licence; elsewhere in England it varies by authority; Scotland and Wales have their own regimes. Check yours. |
| Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 | Lamps and leads inspected and maintained; faults removed from use. |
| Consumer Rights Act 2015 | Reasonable care and skill; clear pricing; no unrealistic guarantees. |
| Equality Act 2010 | No unlawful discrimination; reasonable adjustments. |

Insurance is not a law but it is a condition of trading: confirm in writing that your policy covers builder gel, your qualification route, your premises and, if you teach, your students and models.

## Advertising

Hold evidence for any objective claim. Avoid "guaranteed chip-free", "repairs damaged nails", "allergy-free", "treats fungus", and "qualified nail technician" on the strength of a CPD course alone.
