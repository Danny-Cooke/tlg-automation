## The structures you must protect

The nail unit is more than the visible plate. The **matrix** produces the plate and must never be filed, cut or probed. The **nail plate** has no nerves or blood vessels and can be thinned by over-filing. The **nail bed** is living, vascular tissue beneath it. The **proximal and lateral nail folds** are living skin; filing into them breaks the barrier. The **cuticle** is the thin non-living tissue carried onto the plate; only loose cuticle is removed. The **eponychium** is the living seal, often wrongly called the cuticle, and is not cut. The **hyponychium** is the living barrier under the free edge.

A fingernail grows at around 3 mm a month, so a full nail takes four to six months to replace. Use a range when you explain this to clients.

## Observe, ask, decide, record, refer

A nail professional observes, records, adapts, postpones and refers. A nail professional does not diagnose. When a nail does not look like a healthy nail:

1. **Observe** the nail and surrounding skin.
2. **Ask** when it began, whether it hurts or itches, whether it is changing.
3. **Decide** whether to proceed, adapt or postpone, within your competence and insurance.
4. **Record** the decision, and **refer** when there is infection, inflammation, unexplained change, pain, broken skin or uncertainty.

## Conditions you will meet

Chapter 4 of the manual has photographs of each. The short version:

- **Fungal infection** (thickened, crumbly, discoloured plate): do not apply or reapply. Medical, not a salon service.
- **Onycholysis** (plate separating from the bed): do not cover active or unexplained separation.
- **Paronychia** (red, swollen, tender fold, maybe pus): refuse treatment, advise prompt medical assessment.
- **Green nail** (green-black under lifting): bacterial colonisation in a moist space, not "green fungus". Stop, remove safely, keep dry, refer. Never buff away the colour.
- **A new or changing dark band** along the plate: do not cover; urgent GP assessment.
- **Psoriasis, Beau's lines, white spots, splinter haemorrhages, brittle nails**: often workable when stable and diagnosed; refer when new, painful, spreading or unexplained.

## Contraindications that are not diagnoses

Known acrylate allergy, active eczema or dermatitis on the hands, cuts or broken skin around the nail, warts in the treatment area, recent surgery or severe damage, a client who cannot keep hands safely in the lamp or give informed consent. A GP letter does not make an unsuitable treatment safe.
