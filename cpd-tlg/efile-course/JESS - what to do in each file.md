## 1. E01 Electric File Nail Technique Learner Manual v1.0.docx (submit-to-cpd)

*Page numbers are from the v1.0 PDF copy in working-files (85 pages); Word will be within a page of these.*

- Page 1: 'Version 1.0, November 2026', the scope boundary (electric file on healthy nails and on products the learner already applies; no application teaching, no thickened or diseased nails, no hard skin, no podiatry) and the author paragraph ('more than 400 learners'). Correct anything that is wrong.
- Page 3, Course title: 'Electric File (E-File) Nail Technique CPD Course'. If you would rather it were 'E-File Nail Technique CPD Course' or 'Electric File Nail Services CPD Course', say so and it changes everywhere (manual, pack, website, application). The CPD Group wants 'CPD Course' in it and no 'Level'.
- Page 3, the note on names: the course says 'electric file', never 'drill', and explains why. If you use 'drill' with clients, this is the place to decide.
- Page 3, Entry requirements: Level 2 plus a qualification or CPD certificate in at least one of gel polish, builder gel, hard gel or acrylic, with current experience of applying and removing it. The course works on those products and does not teach them. Is that the learner you want? The acrylate sensitivity bullet now says 'discuss with the school and your doctor' rather than a ban, because dust exposure can be controlled but not removed.
- Page 4, Duration table: 1.5 hours pre-course, 7.5 on the day, about 6 for case studies, 15 in all. Matches E05 and the application text below. If you change them, change all three.
- Pages 5 to 6, the seven safety-critical criteria. A learner fails the practical for any of these regardless of score. Read them as the person who marks it; keep E04 the same.
- Page 5, Models: the model must arrive wearing gel polish, builder gel, hard gel or acrylic at least two weeks old, so there is product to reduce or remove. Re-application afterwards is the learner's own system and not assessed. Decide whether you want the school to put product on practice fingers in advance (the lesson plan says you do) so learners practise reduction before they touch the model.
- Pages 7 to 9, Chapter 2: what the electric file is and is not, benefits and limits, hand file or machine, the history paragraph and 'Service time, pricing and promotion' (acrylic removal forty minutes by hand against fifteen to twenty with the machine; builder gel infill reduction ten; gel polish to the base coat five). Check the timings against what you do. No £ figures on purpose.
- Page 11, Figure 1 is the re-lettered nail unit from the Gel Polish course (the one the assessor asked for larger labels on), at the same size. Print it once and check it reads.
- Pages 15 to 25, Chapter 4: every row of the conditions table now says what the electric file may and may not do (fungal nail: no filing, the dust spreads it; onycholysis: never a bit under the lifted plate; thickened toenails: out of scope, podiatrist). Read the action column; it is yours.
- Pages 29 to 31, Chapter 6: the machine. Figure 13 is a generic unit (speed display, dial, power, forward/reverse, hand/foot). The pre-use check (page 30) and 'Choosing a machine' (page 31) say a professional machine has a 3/32-inch chuck, forward and reverse and torque at low speed, and that hobby rotary tools and unbranded 'nail drills' with 1/8-inch chucks are not for client work. If you recommend a particular machine on the day, the manual deliberately names none.
- Pages 32 to 36, Chapter 7: bit materials, shapes, grits and the task table. The grit colour code is the dental ISO code for diamond bits (black, green, blue, red, yellow, white) with a warning that carbide rings vary by maker. Check the task table (page 35) against the bits you actually hand out. The 'stone' row says cuticle stones are porous and not used on this course; change if you use them.
- Pages 37 to 41, Chapter 8: the physics. The four things you control, the speed table, holding the handpiece, direction of rotation (Figure 17: most right-handed professionals work in forward and switch for the far sidewall), the heat check, rings of fire, reading the dust. This is the chapter the assessor will read most closely. Read it as a learner; if any rule is not what you teach, say so.
- Pages 46 to 47, Chapter 9: two new sections, 'Dust from the electric file' (extraction first, then less dust, then a fitted FFP2/FFP3; a loose paper mask is not RPE) and 'Vibration, noise and the hands' (HAVS and the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations). Make sure the extraction and masks in the room match what it says.
- Pages 48 to 53, Chapter 10: the legislation table has PUWER 1998 (work equipment), the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 and the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations 2008 added, and the TPO row removed (not relevant to the machine). The electrical checklist (page 53) is now about the machine. It also says many insurers ask for evidence of e-file training: check that is true of your insurer and tell learners who it is.
- Pages 54 to 56, Chapter 11: natural-nail preparation. Nine steps; the natural free edge is shaped with a hand file, not the machine; the safety bit never above 8 000 rpm in the cuticle area; de-shining at 3 000 to 5 000 rpm with a 240 band. If you shape the natural free edge with the machine, this chapter and E04 criterion 14 need changing.
- Pages 57 to 59, Chapter 12: reducing and removing each product, with the rule 'leave a thin layer over the plate; the last layer is soaked or hand-filed'. Gel polish goes down to the base coat; the text allows keeping the base where the system is designed for it. Toenails: healthy nails only. Check the sequences against how you do it.
- Pages 60 to 62, Chapter 13: shaping and finishing cured product; backfills are shown but not assessed. If you want backfills taught and assessed, say so; it needs an acrylic-qualified learner and a criterion in E04.
- Pages 63 to 64, Chapter 14: machine care, the checks table and the bit decontamination sequence (Figure 22). Check the disinfectant and contact time you use, and whether your bits are autoclaved.
- Pages 65 to 66, Chapter 15 troubleshooting; pages 67 to 71, Chapters 16 and 17 (contra-actions now include friction heat, rings of fire, bit grabs, dust in the eye; aftercare rewritten for after a removal).
- Pages 72 to 73, Chapter 18: the recap and the 'next steps' paragraph naming the Builder Gel, Acrylic and Gel Polish courses. Check course names.
- Pages 76 to 82, Chapter 20: Form A now asks what is on the nails, who applied it and when, and whether the client has reduced feeling in the fingertips; Form G is a machine and bit record. Read them as the forms you will hand out.
- Then read the whole manual once, start to finish, as a learner would. It was assembled by a script; if a heading, table or picture looks out of place, say so and it gets fixed.

**Brief description (paste):**

> The Electric File (E-File) Nail Technique CPD Course is a one-day, classroom-based skills course for qualified nail professionals who already apply and remove at least one nail coating or enhancement system and want to use an electric file safely and to a professional standard. You will revisit the anatomy of the nail unit and the conditions that mean the electric file must not be used; learn how the machine works (parts, speed, torque, direction, chuck and shank), the bit materials, shapes and grits and what each is for, and how speed, pressure, angle and time on one spot produce friction heat and damage and how to prevent it; and learn, through tutor demonstration and supervised practice on cured product and on a model, to prepare the natural nail with a safety bit, to reduce and remove gel polish, builder gel, hard gel and acrylic without reaching the nail plate, and to shape, refine and finish an enhancement. Dust control, vibration, electrical safety, the decontamination of bits, consultation, UK legislation, aftercare and honest advertising run throughout. Learning is by pre-course reading, tutor demonstration, supervised practice and three post-course case studies, and is assessed by a written theory paper and an observed practical. Entry requirements: aged 18 or over, with a Level 2 Beauty Therapy, Nail Services or equivalent qualification covering manicure, nail structure and safe salon practice, and a qualification or CPD certificate in at least one of gel polish, builder gel, hard gel or liquid-and-powder acrylic with current experience of applying and removing it.

**Learning outcomes (paste, one per line):**

> Explain what an electric file is, what it can and cannot do, its benefits and limitations, and when a hand file is the right tool.
> Identify the structures of the nail unit and surrounding skin, and recognise conditions that mean the electric file must not be used or the service adapted, postponed or refused.
> Conduct and record a professional consultation, obtain informed consent and plan an electric-file service around the product on the nail.
> Describe the parts and controls of an electric file (speed, torque, direction, chuck and shank), and set it up, check it and maintain it safely.
> Select the correct bit material, shape and grit for each task, and explain how speed, pressure, angle and time on one spot produce heat and damage.
> Control dust, vibration and electrical risk in accordance with risk assessment, COSHH, PUWER, the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations and manufacturer instructions, and decontaminate bits correctly between clients.
> Prepare the natural nail with the electric file: cuticle-area work with a safety bit at low speed, and de-shining without thinning the plate.
> Reduce and remove gel polish, builder gel, hard gel and liquid-and-powder acrylic with the electric file, leaving a thin layer over the plate, without heat, grooves or damage.
> Shape, refine and finish an enhancement with the electric file, including the sidewalls, the surface and the underside of the free edge, to a commercial standard.
> Provide aftercare, manage contra-actions and adverse reactions, and recognise and respond to rings of fire, friction heat and an over-thinned plate.
