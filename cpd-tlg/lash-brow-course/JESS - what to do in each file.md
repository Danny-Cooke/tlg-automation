# Lash and Brow Tint and Wax CPD Course: what to do in each file before submitting

For Jess. Prepared 8 November 2026. Folder: `cpd-tlg/lash-brow-course`. This is a new course with no application yet.

## Before you start

- Everything is in cpd-tlg/lash-brow-course. Upload only what is in submit-to-cpd. Nothing in working-files goes to The CPD Group.
- The course was written from scratch to the standard of the Gel Polish course, following The CPD Group's six-step structure. Your old 'Lash & Brow Tint and Wax Beauty Course' PDF was read for scope only and is kept in working-files/source for the record; none of its text or pictures is used. Forty of its 55 pages were generic salon material, its legislation was out of date in several places (Electricity at Work '1992', RIDDOR '1995', PPE '2002', a cosmetics regulation replaced in 2013, a Disability Discrimination Act entry with the wrong text under it) and some steps were unsafe (lower lashes tinted with the eyes open, a patch test at 24 hours, nothing about the under-16 rule, nothing about rinsing an eye). Read the new manual as a new document, not as an edit.
- Work through this list in order. Each heading is one file. Make your changes in Word, save as .docx (not PDF) and keep the file name.
- If you change a theory question in L02, change the same question in L03. If you change a safety-critical criterion in L04, change the list on pages 5 to 6 of L01 to match.
- **The slot.** The contract has one free course slot and the E-File course was written for it on 6 November. Both cannot go in without a further slot: decide which goes first, or ask The CPD Group what a sixth slot costs. Nothing else in this list depends on that decision.

It says you hold the VTCT Level 2 NVQ Diploma in Nail Services and the Award in Education and Training and that you provide lash and brow treatments at The Looking Glass. The CPD Group's 'Expertise' criterion needs the author's qualification in the subject: add the eyelash and eyebrow (or beauty therapy) qualification or certificate you hold, with the awarding body and year, here and on your CV. If you do not hold one, tell Dan before anything is submitted.
- Page 1: check 'Version 1.0, November 2026', the scope boundary (lash tint, brow tint, brow shaping by wax, tweezers and scissors; lifts, lamination, henna, extensions, threading and permanent make-up are out) and the 'more than 400 learners' line.
- Page 3, Course title: 'Lash and Brow Tint and Wax CPD Course', which keeps your own name for the course with 'CPD Course' added as they require. If you would rather it were 'Eyelash and Eyebrow Tinting and Shaping CPD Course' or 'Lash and Brow Treatments CPD Course', say so and it changes everywhere (manual, pack, website, application). No 'HD brows': that is someone's trade mark.
- Page 3, the 'One subject' box: The CPD Group refused the BIAB and gel polish course as two subjects in one. Tinting and shaping are defended here as one treatment group (they are one unit in the industry standards and are booked and done together). If you would rather split them into two applications, say so; it is two slots.
- Page 3, Entry requirements: Level 2 Beauty Therapy or equivalent covering skin, facial and eye-area treatments; aged 18 or over; no previous lash or brow experience needed. Change if you want something different.
- Page 4, Duration table: 1.5 hours pre-course, 7.5 on the day, about 6 for case studies, 15 in all. Matches L05 and the application text below. If you change them, change all three.
- Pages 5 to 6, the seven safety-critical criteria. A learner fails the practical for any of these regardless of score. Read them as the person who marks it; keep L04 the same.
- Page 5, Models: the model must be 18 or over and must have had a negative patch test with the school's tint and developer recorded at least 48 hours before the course; the plan is that models come to the school for a patch-test appointment in the week before. A model without one is shaped but not tinted. Is that how you want to run it?
- Pages 7 to 10, Chapter 2: what the three treatments are and are not, the related services compared (lift, lamination, henna, extensions, threading), a short history and 'Service time, pricing and promotion' (brow wax 15 minutes, brow tint 15 to 20, wax and tint 25 to 30, lash tint 30 to 40, lash and brow tint about 45). Check the timings against what you do. No £ figures on purpose.
- Pages 11 to 18, Chapter 3: the skin, the hair and its follicle, the growth cycle, the eye and lids, the brow and its four muscles, with Figures 1 to 5 (all drawings). Check the numbers you teach: lashes 90 to 160 upper and 70 to 80 lower, lash anagen 4 to 10 weeks, brow cycle 3 to 4 months, a lash tint lasting 4 to 6 weeks and a brow tint 3 to 4.
- Pages 19 to 23, Chapter 4: three contraindication tables (eye conditions; skin conditions; medication and treatments) and the 'not diagnoses' list. Read the action column; it is yours. The waiting times it gives are: retinoid creams stopped seven days before a wax, isotretinoin six months, botulinum toxin and filler fourteen days, peels and laser two to four weeks or until cleared, eye surgery until the surgeon clears it, sun and sunbeds 48 hours. Change any you teach differently.
- Page 19, Figure 6: the four eye conditions (conjunctivitis, stye, blepharitis, eyelid eczema) are drawings, not photographs. On the Gel Polish course the assessor asked for realistic images in the conditions section. Openly licensed clinical photographs can be added under the drawings with their credits, as the nail manuals have; say the word and it is done.
- Page 21, under-16s: the manual says no tint under 16 because the products carry that warning by law, and parental consent plus your insurer's agreement at 16 and 17. Check your insurer's actual minimum age and tell Dan if it is 18 for everything.
- Pages 24 to 27, Chapter 5: the consultation sequence and the patch test. The school's rule is written as **at least 48 hours** before the first tint, with the exact tint and developer, repeated for every new brand, after six months, and after any change in health, medication or pregnancy, and never for a client who has reacted to hair dye or a black henna tattoo. Fresha and your old manual say 24 hours. Your insurer's wording decides: if it says 24, tell Dan and the manual, the pack and the application text change together; 48 is the safer default.
- Pages 28 to 30, Chapter 6: how tint works, the developer at about 3% (10 volume), shade and timing (lashes 5 to 15 minutes, brows checked at one minute, 3 to 5 at most), why it sensitises, and the legal position of the products. If the brand you use gives different ratios or times, this is where to say; the manual names no brand on purpose.
- Pages 31 to 34, Chapter 7: mapping with the three lines from the side of the nostril (Figure 9) and the five shapes (Figure 10). This is your method from the old manual, drawn properly. Check it is how you teach it.
- Pages 35 to 37, Chapter 8: the kit lists. Hot (peelable) wax is written as the default for the eye area with strip wax allowed under the same rules. If you teach strip wax first, say so and the order changes. Check the shade list (light brown, brown, dark brown, graphite, chestnut, black, blue-black) against what you stock.
- Pages 38 to 40, Chapter 9: hygiene, the between-client sequence, the blood-spot procedure, posture. Make sure the saline pods, the lidded heaters and the autoclave it mentions are what is in the room.
- Pages 41 to 45, Chapter 10: the legislation table (18 rows, current to November 2026, including the Cosmetics Regulation's conditions for eyelash tints and the 2024 Act that replaced the unfair-trading regulations), insurance, data protection, and the ASA table (no 'permanent mascara', no 'makes lashes grow', no 'hypoallergenic', no 'no patch test needed'). Read the insurance section against your policy.
- Pages 46 to 47, Chapter 11: preparing the client, and the order **tint, tidy, shape** (brows tinted before they are waxed), which is the order in your old manual. If you wax first, say so; the reasoning in the box changes too.
- Pages 48 to 50, Chapter 12: lash tinting, twelve steps, eyes closed and relaxed from the first pad to the last wipe (your old manual had the lower lashes done with the eyes open; that is gone). Read it as a learner; if any step is not what you do, say so.
- Pages 51 to 52, Chapter 13: brow tinting, checked at one minute and built in stages, one step lighter on a first visit.
- Pages 53 to 55, Chapter 14: brow shaping. Temperature test on your wrist then the client's skin, one spatula per dip, with the growth on and against it off, skin taut, parallel, one pass, never the mobile eyelid, above the brow strays only; then tweezing and trimming. The section order (under outer, under inner, between, above) is a suggestion: change it to yours.
- Pages 56 to 61, Chapters 15 and 16: troubleshooting, contra-actions, and tint in the eye (Figure 13: saline from the inner corner outward for at least ten minutes, refer if anything persists). Check the first-aid steps match your training.
- Pages 62 to 63, Chapter 17: aftercare and the client takeaway box. This is what your written aftercare card should say.
- Pages 64 to 65, Chapter 18: the recap and 'Continuing your development', which names lash lifting and brow lamination as next steps and the four nail courses. Check course names.
- Pages 68 to 74, Chapter 20: Forms A to H. Form A is the lash and brow consultation (health questions, patch-test block, assessment table, plan, consent with a parent's signature line); Form G is the patch-test record. Read them as the forms you will hand out.
- Then read the whole manual once, start to finish, as a learner would. It was assembled by a script; if a heading, table or picture looks out of place, say so and it gets fixed.

> The Lash and Brow Tint and Wax CPD Course is a one-day, classroom-based skills course for qualified beauty professionals who want to offer eyelash tinting, eyebrow tinting and eyebrow shaping safely and to a professional standard. You will revisit the anatomy of the skin, the hair and its growth cycle, the eye, the eyelids and the eyebrow, and the conditions that mean a treatment is adapted, postponed or refused; learn how an oxidative tint colours the hair, why it can sensitise, how shade, developer and timing decide the result, and why the patch test, the age limit and the eye protection are non-negotiable; and learn, through tutor demonstration and supervised practice on each other and on a model, to tint lashes and brows with the eyes protected and closed, and to map, wax, tweeze and trim the brows to a shape agreed with the client. **Learning outcomes (paste, one per line):**

> Explain what eyelash and eyebrow tinting and eyebrow shaping are, their benefits and limitations, who they suit and when another service or a referral is the honest answer.
> Describe the structure and growth cycle of the hair, the skin of the eye area and the anatomy of the eye, eyelids and eyebrow, and recognise the conditions that mean a treatment is adapted, postponed or refused.
> Conduct and record a professional consultation, carry out and record a patch test, obtain informed consent and agree an individual treatment plan.
> Explain how an oxidative tint colours the hair, why it can sensitise, how shade, developer and timing decide the result, and why the products are restricted to professional use and to clients aged 16 and over.
> Select, handle, store and dispose of tints, developers, waxes and equipment in accordance with risk assessment, COSHH, manufacturer instructions and current UK requirements.
> Prepare the client and the treatment area, protect the eyes and skin, and work hygienically throughout.
> Tint eyelashes to an even, natural-looking result with the eyes protected and closed, no tint on the skin and no tint in the eye.
> Tint eyebrows to a shade that suits the client, timed and checked, with the skin kept clean.
> Map, wax, tweeze and trim the eyebrows to a balanced shape agreed with the client, with the wax temperature tested, the skin supported and the eyelid never waxed.
> Provide aftercare, recognise and manage contra-actions, adverse reactions and tint in the eye, and set honest expectations about results and how long they last.
